## 2. Six-stage sequence

| Stage | Stage name | Core focus |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Read and Understand | Main idea, details, sequence, inference and evidence in short fiction and nonfiction |
| Stage 2 | Vocabulary in Context | Word meaning, antonyms, shades of meaning, roots, affixes, collocations and word choice |
| Stage 3 | Grammar and Sentence Accuracy | Error identification, sentence correction, punctuation, syntax and editing |
| Stage 4 | Paragraph Logic and Improvement | Sentence order, cohesion, transitions, relevance, concision and paragraph revision |
| Stage 5 | Inference and Critical Reading | Interpretation, authorial purpose, tone, assumptions, bias, evidence and source quality |
| Stage 6 | Synthesis and Independent Expression | Summaries, comparisons, integrated evidence, literary responses, nonfiction analysis and independent writing |

All six stages use the same stage names at every level. Level-specific outcomes are listed in Section 7.

## 3. Explore and Extend boundaries

### Explore
- Fiction comprehension uses short stories only; do not use novel extracts as comprehension passages.
- Use three short stories in Stages 1, 3 and 5, and two short stories in Stages 2, 4 and 6.
- Every story has at least 20 distinct comprehension questions in the stage bank.
- Include two age-appropriate nonfiction passages per stage, with at least 20 comprehension questions per passage.
- Build foundational practice in each of the other ten question types.

### Extend
- Do not classify short-story comprehension or novel excerpts as novel comprehension.
- Include one age-appropriate nonfiction passage per stage, with at least 20 comprehension questions.
- Develop higher-order applications of the same skill families.
- Each level includes three candidate novels. Novel 1 is read and studied across Stages 1–2; Novel 2 across Stages 3–4; Novel 3 across Stages 5–6.
- Novel activities focus on the whole book: character development, decisions and consequences, themes, vocabulary, narrative choices, evidence-based interpretation, comparisons and extended responses. Do not turn excerpts from novels into passage-comprehension activities.

The three novels per level are a reading pathway, not three novels assigned to every stage. Validate individual titles for readability, length, age suitability and public-domain rights before adoption.

### Question-type design requirements

- **Short-story comprehension:** detail, main idea, sequence, cause and effect, character/motive, inference, theme, authorial purpose, tone and textual evidence, calibrated to grade.
- **Nonfiction comprehension:** passages about science, nature, biography, history, inventions, culture and Indian society; include claims, supporting details, fact/opinion, conclusions and evidence quality as levels advance.
- **Vocabulary and antonyms:** meanings in context, near-synonyms, shades of meaning, word families, affixes, collocations, connotations and context-sensitive opposites.
- **Error identification:** identify the location and type of a grammatical error; each item must have a defensible answer.
- **Sentence correction:** choose or create an accurate, clear revision while preserving intended meaning.
- **Paragraph improvement:** use original items inspired by older SAT-style writing tasks; improve relevance, order, transitions, cohesion, concision, logic, tone or conclusion. Do not copy SAT questions.
- **Sentence construction:** reorder sentences, combine related ideas, remove irrelevant material and create clear sentences or paragraphs.
- **Inference and evidence:** select or cite evidence and explain interpretations. Assign each item one primary type for analytics even when skills overlap.
- **Written responses:** summaries, explanations, comparisons, literary interpretations, reviews and arguments. Evaluate open responses with rubrics and accept defensible alternatives.
- **Listening/oral responses:** listening comprehension, retelling and evidence-supported oral explanation where the learning experience supports these formats.

The five mandatory types—paragraph improvement, nonfiction comprehension, antonyms, error identification and sentence correction—must appear in both Explore and Extend at every level, with age-adjusted language and cognitive demands. All six curriculum strands in ENGLISH_APTITUDE_CURRICULUM.md must be assessed across the stage; do not force every skill into each individual story.

These totals count the distinct question-bank items described in Section 4: story questions, nonfiction questions and the ten other question types. Novel study is included through relevant Extend inference and written-response work; novel extracts do not add fiction-comprehension items.

### Question-bank versus activity delivery

The bank sizes above are distinct authored-item targets, not the number of items shown in one sitting. Retain a minimum of five Explore and three Extend activities per stage, with a 10-question attempt as a session format, but do not truncate the larger bank to fit one attempt. Activity packaging must allow suitable distribution, rotation or repeat practice so that the approved question pool remains usable. The exact implementation of sets and delivery belongs to the later implementation-roadmap step.

Assign every question one primary question-type label so analytics can report type, Explore/Extend and stage totals without double-counting.

## 7. Level-specific expected outcomes

### Level 1 — Grade 3
1. Read and Understand: identify characters, setting, explicit details and simple event sequence.
2. Vocabulary in Context: use familiar context clues and straightforward antonyms.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: recognise sentence boundaries, capitals, basic punctuation, common verb forms and simple agreement.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: order short sentences, identify a topic sentence and choose a clearer or relevant sentence.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: infer feelings from clear clues and distinguish fact from opinion in familiar nonfiction.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: retell a story, write a short summary and discuss a character or reading choice.

### Level 2 — Grade 4
1. Read and Understand: identify main ideas, supporting details, event sequence and straightforward motives.
2. Vocabulary in Context: interpret multiple-meaning words, antonyms, prefixes, suffixes and word families.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: improve agreement, tense, pronouns, articles, prepositions and punctuation.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: identify sentence order, supporting details, repetition and transitions.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: support an inference with clues and identify simple cause/effect in fiction and nonfiction.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: summarise coherently and explain a character's decision using textual clues.

### Level 3 — Grade 5
1. Read and Understand: summarise stories, separate main ideas from details and understand straightforward implied meaning.
2. Vocabulary in Context: apply roots, affixes, synonyms, antonyms and contextual meaning.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: correct tense, pronoun, agreement and punctuation errors; recognise compound sentences and conjunctions.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: improve coherence, combine related sentences and remove misplaced or irrelevant information.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: support interpretations with textual evidence and recognise claims and evidence.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: compare short texts and write supported responses explaining character choices or changes.

### Level 4 — Grade 6
1. Read and Understand: summarise multiple paragraphs, identify authorial purpose and recognise basic text structures.
2. Vocabulary in Context: interpret roots, affixes, shades of meaning and context-dependent antonyms.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: work with clauses, connectives, sentence boundaries, agreement and tense consistency.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: improve transitions, sentence placement, relevance, redundancy and paragraph structure.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: explain implied motives, interpret accessible tone and assess evidence for nonfiction claims.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: write evidence-supported paragraphs and compare how two texts treat an idea.

### Level 5 — Grade 7
1. Read and Understand: interpret character development, identify themes and produce balanced summaries.
2. Vocabulary in Context: distinguish connotation, collocation, figurative use and context-sensitive antonyms.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: improve clauses, modifiers, pronoun clarity, punctuation and sentence variety.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: improve logical flow, introductions, conclusions, transitions and sentence combinations.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: interpret tone, recognise introductory forms of irony and evaluate supporting evidence.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: compare viewpoints and write structured claims supported by evidence.

### Level 6 — Grade 8
1. Read and Understand: connect unstated ideas, examine narrative perspective and explain how structure affects meaning.
2. Vocabulary in Context: interpret idioms, precise word choice, figurative language, collocations and register.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: edit subordinate clauses, modifier placement, parallel structure and tense consistency.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: improve cohesion, concision, sentence placement and logical relationships.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: evaluate purpose, counterpoints, evidence quality, bias and fact/opinion.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: synthesise two texts and produce thematic or comparative responses with precise evidence.

### Level 7 — Grade 9
1. Read and Understand: interpret symbolism, tone shifts, narrative perspective and less obvious implications.
2. Vocabulary in Context: distinguish denotation, connotation, register and nuanced word relationships.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: diagnose complex errors involving syntax, modifiers, parallelism, agreement and clauses.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: evaluate order, relevance, transitions, concision and rhetorical clarity.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: analyse assumptions, claims, evidence, bias and source reliability.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: formulate an interpretive thesis and compare authorial approaches using evidence.

### Level 8 — Grade 10
1. Read and Understand: interpret ambiguity, subtext, irony and structural choices in short fiction.
2. Vocabulary in Context: evaluate nuanced word choice, connotation, register, collocations and context-sensitive opposites.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: edit complex syntax, emphasis, tense consistency, modifiers and punctuation.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: revise structure, logic, tone, cohesion, emphasis and concision.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: evaluate rhetoric, counterarguments, source reliability, limitations and competing perspectives.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: create multi-text responses that consider alternatives and use precise evidence.

### Level 9 — Grades 11–12
1. Read and Understand: conduct close reading of complex short stories, examining ambiguity, structure, tone and purpose.
2. Vocabulary in Context: analyse nuanced antonyms, connotation, metaphor, register and advanced academic vocabulary.
3. Grammar and Sentence Accuracy: evaluate sophisticated syntax, grammatical precision and the difference between a genuine error and a stylistic choice.
4. Paragraph Logic and Improvement: improve coherence, emphasis, concision, rhetorical effectiveness and sustained argument flow.
5. Inference and Critical Reading: evaluate assumptions, evidence, bias, perspective and source credibility across nonfiction.
6. Synthesis and Independent Expression: produce nuanced critical responses, synthesise evidence across texts and create independent literary or argumentative writing.

Grade 12 should demonstrate greater depth and independence than Grade 11 without a separate product-access level.

## 8. Assessment and question-quality rules

- Track comprehension, vocabulary, grammar, paragraph improvement, written response and reading engagement as distinguishable outcomes rather than relying solely on a single combined score.
- Assess open responses for understanding, evidence, reasoning, organisation and language accuracy. Accept defensible alternatives where answers can reasonably vary.
- Every question must be age-appropriate, unambiguous, answerable from the intended text or language rule, and supplied with an appropriate answer key or scoring rubric.
- Review distractors for plausibility and remove answer-length clues, duplicate options, accidental ambiguity and repeated content.
- Correct answers must not habitually be the longest or most elaborately worded options.
- Choose source texts and passages with cultural relevance for Indian learners while reviewing older works for dated language, stereotypes, colonial assumptions and sensitive content.
- Apply the copyright and edition-verification policy in ENGLISH_APTITUDE_CURRICULUM.md to every story, passage, novel, translation, illustration and adaptation.
